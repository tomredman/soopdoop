// soopdoop for crews

Stop doing the same work twice.

For a crew of up to 8 people, each running 3 to 10 coding agents. Every agent asks the agent that already knows. Work goes to the agent that knows the area. And the crew stays in touch without sitting in Slack.

See the plans

macOS 14+ · Superset · Claude Code, Codex, Grok · free for friends · $10 a member a month for teams

// the four numbers

Four numbers say what it did.

The HUD, the menu bar and the weekly digest show your crew the same four numbers. They count only what soopdoop did, for the whole crew, never per person.

Sample week

  • 212questions answered by crewmates’ agents · about 9M tokens saved
  • 14hand-offs done by the agent that knew the area
  • 3heads-ups acted on: duplicate or clashing work caught early
  • 27ships this week, 9 with walkthroughs

Made-up numbers for a crew of four. “Tokens saved” is an estimate and never a score. The other three count real events.

// what a crew gets

Six things, one screen each.

Each one is shown with a made-up crew (@tom, @adalovelace, @jimmy and @grace) building a taco app.

01

Every agent on the wire

Most crews mix tools. Claude Code, Codex and Grok agents show up side by side, with a badge for each. Every one of them can answer the crew’s questions and take a hand-off.

  • Setup puts soopdoop’s hooks next to Superset’s, for each tool on your Mac.
  • A question is answered by a throwaway copy of the agent’s session, on its owner’s own account.
  • Private folders never show up.
02

The ledger

When a session ends, its owner’s Mac writes one line about it: what was done, where, how many files, and the pull request. Together the lines are the crew’s memory of who did what. Ask who knows about something, and the Operator answers from the ledger, even when that person’s agents are off.

  • At most 240 characters. Keys and tokens are taken out.
  • You see your own lines in the HUD, and you can rewrite or delete any of them.
  • Kept 7 days on Free and 90 days on Team. Never shown above the crew.
03

Heads-ups and hand-offs

At every prompt, your Mac checks it against what your crewmates’ agents are on. If one of them is deep in that area already, you get a card before you start, and your agent gets one line: ask that agent, or hand it the task.

A hand-off runs in your crewmate’s agent, on their account, once they say yes. They see your exact words and which of their agents gets them.

  • The check runs on your Mac, with no model, and sends your prompt nowhere.
  • Each person picks, per crewmate: ask me each time (the default), run when my agent is idle, or never.
  • Hand-offs have their own budget on the Mac that runs them, $10 a day unless you change it, and a log there.
  • A card lasts 10 minutes. An offer lasts 24 hours.
04

Crew notes

Short shared instructions: design rules, pricing facts, decisions. A note reaches every running agent on every tool, so nobody’s agent works from last month’s rules.

  • Up to 400 characters. “Applies when” sends a note only to sessions in one folder or repository.
  • An agent can propose a note. Its owner approves it with one click.
  • After 30 days the author is asked “still true?”. A note nobody confirms stops reaching agents after 45 days.
  • 10 live notes on Free, 60 on Team.
05

The room and the Slack digest

The wire is the crew’s room: ships, hand-offs done, new notes, shouts, and props, also from customers. Every day at your crew’s hour (9:00 unless you change it), the Operator writes “Yesterday in” your crew and posts it to your Slack channel. On Mondays come the week’s four numbers.

  • Positive only. Never “idle since”, never a ranking.
  • A shout is one line, gone after 24 hours.
  • Props from customers come from Intercom ratings, or from the scout on your own Mac. Only one short line reaches the wire.
  • The leader pastes a Slack webhook into Settings. The live mirror, off until turned on, posts ships, props and saves as they happen.
06

The menu bar

The menu bar is the glance. One dot per crewmate: green working, blue idle, grey off. Open it for a line per crewmate, yesterday’s digest, hand-offs waiting for you, and three small boxes: Ask the crew, Hand off, Shout. None of them needs the HUD.

  • Each line shows only what that crewmate shares.
  • The icon pulses purple while a question is in flight, and counts the hand-offs waiting for you.
  • A knock turns it into a bell.

// what leaves your mac

Your conversations stay on your Mac.

  1. A conversation never leaves your Mac. Three short things made from it do: the routing summary (one line per open agent), the ledger line (one line per finished task), and the answer your agent writes to one crewmate’s question. Each is made on your Mac, shown to you, and kept inside your crew.
  2. Prompts are read on your Mac for heads-ups, and that check sends them nowhere.
  3. Nothing runs on your account without your say. A hand-off needs your yes for that crewmate, shows its exact words first, runs on your plan, and is logged on your Mac.
  4. Private agents’ work never leaves your Mac. No summary, no ledger line, no heads-up, no hand-off, and nobody but you sees them.
  5. Above the crew: work only. No names, no counts, no ledger lines.

Every field, who sees it and how long it is kept: the privacy page.

// start a crew

One line. About a minute.

Terminal
  1. Run the line on each Mac. It installs soopdoop, builds the Mac app on that Mac, and starts it.
  2. Sign in with Superset in the HUD, and pick a handle.
  3. Invite your crew. Menu bar → Invite someone new. The first invite someone accepts starts your crew, and you lead it.
  4. Go Team when it helps. Settings → Crew → Upgrade. The leader pays; everyone in the crew gets it.

Each Mac needs

  • macOS 14+
  • a Superset account
  • Claude Code
  • Xcode command line tools

Setup connects Codex and Grok too, when they are on the Mac.
